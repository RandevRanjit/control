Screens

The pull request in Git, and the screens version 1 left in T3's look: Settings, Usage, Welcome, the agent sign-in page and the thread's details. Drawn 27 Oct 2026 for the owner's approval (P3, P4).

Version 1 drew the window; these are the places you go from it. Each is drawn as D: the same tokens, faces, rows and controls, so nothing in the app still looks like T3. The words are the product's: a thread, not a chat.

SC1Git holds the pull request

Pull requests were in four places: the panel's Pull request and Linked pull requests surfaces, ⌘K's Link pull request, and a page of their own. They fold into one: PR, the third mode of the Git tab after Graph and Diff. It opens on this thread's pull request: its state, title and branches, its checks and reviews, then its files against the base. Merge, Ready and Open act on it. Under it, the pull requests linked to this thread, a stack's layers among them, and the repository's other open ones; a click on any shows it here. The field at the foot links another by its link or number; ⌘K's Link pull request opens this tab with the field ready. The Pull Requests page goes.

SC1cEvery project's pull requests, in the same view

The Pull Requests page folds in too (owner, 27 Oct). Under this thread's pull request, PR lists the pull requests of this repository or of every project, grouped as the page groups them: review requested from you, yours, others. The row is the page's own, so nothing is redrawn and nothing is lost: state over checks, number and title, the diff on the right; author, repository and review under it, the age on the right. The view fits the panel's width, which the panel already lets you change: drag its edge, or maximise it. At 440 the filters sit behind one icon, with a count while any is set. Widened past 600, the filters come out as pickers, rows show their labels, and rows can be picked and closed together.

SC2Settings

The gear holds down and the sidebar becomes Settings' pages, under the same top row and a search field. A page is a column of groups: each row a label with a line under it, and its control at the right, a picker, a switch or a number. Every page takes these rows. The layers mark after a label says a project can override it, as now. Three pages wore another meaning's icon (§20): Providers drew Agents' bot, Integrations Plugins' blocks and Source control the branch. Here Providers becomes Claude, the only provider, with the spark; Integrations takes a puzzle piece and Source control a fork. Page names are written properly: Scheduled tasks, Source control.

SC3Usage

The chart at the sidebar's foot opens it, and holds down. The page keeps everything it has now, in D's skin: the head's environments and providers, its three views, Cost, Tokens and Limits, and its period. Cost leads with the total and each provider's share, beside the daily line; then the totals, cost by type, and the breakdown by model or by day, ranked, each with its share underlined. Tokens is the same page counted in tokens, with tokens by type. Limits are the sidebar's 5H and 7D lines at full width: how much is used, never how much is left, the pace, and when each resets. Claude keeps its own orange mark wherever the page names it.

SC4Welcome

The first run is one float sheet on the bare field, three steps along its top: Connect, Agents, Projects. The stair logo heads it, not the word.

SC5Agent sign-in

When an agent outside CONTROL signs in, this page opens in your browser. T3's blue gradient goes: the page is the field with the stair logo and one raised card. The permission modes are the composer's, with their padlocks (§20.2); Read only takes the eye.

SC6The thread's details

The details toggle in the head opens the thread's card: where it runs and what you can do there. Its computer and folder, the editor, its scripts; then its worktree, publishing and what changed. Changes opens Git.
