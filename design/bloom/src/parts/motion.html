Motion

One clock: 1.75, 3.5, 7 and 14 s.

The reel loops because every period in it is a power of two of 7 seconds. CONTROL takes the clock, not the flower: every loop runs at 1.75, 3.5, 7 or 14 s from one shared instant, so the window breathes as one object. Each session now moves in two places at once. Its lamp carries the motion, in ink. Its state bar carries the colour, flowing at the state's tempo on the same clock. Linear means work, sine means waiting, still means nothing to do.

M1One clock

Every loop counts from one instant: host time floored to a 14 s boundary. Host time is shared by every process on the Mac, so the main window, the menu-bar panel and any helper land on the same phase grid without talking to each other. A loop starts one 14 s cycle early, so a lamp or a bar that appears late is already in step: nothing fills in, and nothing stores a phase. One-shots never wait for the beat. A change shows on the frame it's caused; only its loop falls into step.

M2Five curves

Space is sine and colour is linear, as in the reel. The rule that falls out of it: linear means work, sine means waiting, still means nothing to do. Working, Background, housekeeping and the loader are linear lamps: a machine doing something. Needs-you, done, failed, choose, outside and idle are sine: a thing waiting, breathing. Gone is still.

Every state bar is colour travel, so every bar is linear. What tells a waiting bar from a working one is its tempo, and the lamp beside it.

M3Every lamp and bar, live and still

Urgency is ranked by four levers, most salient first: reach (does the motion leave the lamp?), tempo, channel (scale, then travel, then fill level, then opacity) and hue. The lamp carries the first three in ink, so every eye reads state the same way, colour-blind or not. The bead bar before it adds the fourth: blue working, amber failed, green done, and red for needs-you alone.

In a row the two sit together at the right: a run of beads a sixth of the row wide, then the lamp at the far end as its cap. Read right to left, the lamp says what's happening and the beads say it in colour. Each bar is one smooth wave inside its state's family, base to light to base to deep, so the colour flows through the beads with no band in it. Needs-you alone runs at 1.75 s; failed and done at 3.5 s; working, Background and choose at 7 s; housekeeping at 14 s. Idle, outside and gone hold still. Working and Background share one bar; the lamp tells them apart. Only the one session in focus keeps a continuous bar, at the top of the view.

M4A sidebar on the clock

Five rows run a script on the shared clock. Each frame is computed from where the clock is, so a scrub or a ?t= shot lands on the exact state, and a jump never plays a transition. State never waits; only position may lag, by one T/16 at most. Every bar of one state shares one phase, so the working bars here flow as one.

M5Fire any change

A change of state is the reel's rest → stripes → settle, played in two places at once. In the lamp, the new shape is drawn and an ink band sweeps up through it. In the bar, the old ramp is wiped off left to right by its bridge, while the new ramp is already flowing underneath, in phase since the first frame. A bar on its own never holds a bridge: it stays inside one family, so it blends. A change crosses families, so it bridges. Most changes pass through cream: a bright, ordinary change. A calm state turning into needs-you, or back, passes through navy and wine instead: the system's one dark beat, so a calm session suddenly needing you has its own drop. In a row the wipe runs under the beads, so the bridge crosses them bead by bead.

M7Panels and the selection bar

Ambient motion says state and nothing else: here, the lamps and the state bars. One-shot motion may also answer your hand (press, hover, focus, panels, rows, theme), and it ends within 875 ms. Panels lay out once and then animate the layer: the final frame commits on the first frame, so the terminal reflows exactly once, and the panel's content slides 24 pt and fades in.

M9Theme without a flash

Crossfading every colour per element sends the window through mid-grey mush, where text and ground pass through equal lightness: an unreadable frame, then a jump. So lightness moves one way only, through the bridge. Night → Field passes through cream and only rises; Field → Night passes through navy and only falls. It is the reel's rest → bridge → rest at window scale.

M11Core Animation recipes

Every loop is a render-server animation, committed once on a state change. No main-thread work happens per frame: the only main-thread cost is adding or removing an animation in the transaction that applies a daemon View. Each lamp is at most 3 layers and at most 2 running animations. A state bar is one gradient layer with one running animation, a translate: in a row it slides under a still mask of beads, and the head bar slides in a clipping pill.
