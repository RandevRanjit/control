Shell

T3 as it is, three ways to combine it with Bloom, and D, version 1. Every window is 1512 × 945, drawn at 1:1 and scaled to the column.

A window is five decisions: the sidebar, the head of the conversation, the centre, the composer and the panel. T3 and Bloom disagree on all five. A takes T3's answer to each and paints it in Bloom. B takes Bloom's answer to each, the window you confirmed in section 06, and maps T3's features into it. C picks per decision, and the table says which side wins where and why. S1T3 Code as it is

The baseline, drawn from T3's source at the fork point. The sidebar is one flat list of cards, newest first: the folder on line 1 with a status label at the right, the chat's title on line 2, the branch on line 3. Pinned chats lead the list, snoozed and settled ones drop to shelves at the bottom, and archive is a menu action with no shelf. The head is a breadcrumb, folder / title, with three toggles: details, the shell drawer (⌘J) and the right panel.

The centre is the conversation already. Your messages are bubbles on the right; each turn's tool calls are a work log of one-line rows with an icon each, folding to "Worked for 18s" when the turn ends. The composer carries the model, effort and access pickers, and a strip under it holds where the turn runs: this folder or a new worktree, and the branch. The panel holds tabs you open, rename and close. No status label moves: working is a dashed ring and a ticking time.

S2A · T3's frame, Bloom's paint

T3's geometry kept whole and painted with Bloom: Field and Night, Regrade for the chrome and Plex for anything you could paste, the session's name in Plex on line 2, and tone steps where T3 draws its 1 px rules. Where T3 puts a status label, A puts Bloom's state: a bead bar and the colourless lamp at the right of line 1, with the state words joining the branch on line 3.

It is the cheapest option to build, because every T3 component keeps its layout and takes new tokens. What it gives up is most of what you confirmed in section 06: the cards become T3's icon rows, your prompt becomes a bubble, the head says the title the selected row already says, and a three-line card holds a chat in 82 px where Bloom's row takes 42.

S3B · Bloom's frame on T3's engine

Section 06 as confirmed: the sidebar runs the full height and holds the lights; the head says only the path, over the one full state bar; the centre is the conversation, with every read, write, error and agent as a card; the panel is a strip of icons. T3 brings the engine underneath, so the cards come from T3's own event stream instead of a mod watching a TUI, and Send goes straight to the agent instead of pre-typing into tmux.

T3's six panel views land in Bloom's five icons. Files becomes Folder. Diff and Pull request become Git, with Graph | Diff as its mode and the PR as a row at its top. Browser and Terminal keep their names. Device (T3's simulator mirror for mobile work) has no tab: it opens from the command palette when a project has a simulator. Outputs is Bloom's alone: the files a chat produced.

03

Groups in the sidebar

v2 adds a Group above projects and chats. Three ways to draw the three tiers in one column.

T3's sidebar is one list of chats, each card naming its folder; a legacy setting, off by default, draws the same chats as a folder tree. Its "project grouping" setting only merges one repository seen from two paths or machines; it is not a group you name. v2's Group is yours: a name, any chats from any folder, and an optional home folder that new chats in the group start in. So the sidebar has three tiers to place: group, folder, chat.

All three use the same twelve chats. ORBIT holds PORTFOLIO-SCROLL from the portfolio folder on purpose, because a group that crosses folders is the case a folder list can't draw. Chats that belong to no group sit under Loose. Snoozed chats fold away with their wake time, and Archive keeps its count, so neither costs rows. Pinned is a pin on line 2, and the chat holds the top of its group.

G1Groups › folders › chats · version 1
  • T3's legacy folder tree, kept whole inside each group, so per-folder actions (scripts, worktree base, open in editor) keep a home.
  • Reads like Finder: where the work lives is always visible.
  • A fourth indent and a header per folder: ten rows of chrome for twelve chats, so less fits.
  • A group with one folder (THESIS, CONTROL) repeats itself.
G2Groups › chats, folder on line 2
  • Bloom's own sidebar, unchanged in shape: every row a chat, every lamp and bar in one column.
  • The folder is a bare label after the state words, so a cross-folder group reads at a glance.
  • Line 2 gets one more word; on long state words the folder is what truncates first.
  • Folder actions move to the group's menu and the chat head.
G3One group at a time
  • Each group gets the whole column; its tally pearls still show every other group's states.
  • Fits lock well: a locked group is a pill that won't open.
  • A needs-you in another group is one pearl, not a row: it can't break through the way a row does.
  • One more click between groups; All brings back the long list.

G1–G3Same chats, three tiers.ORBIT-WEBSITE is selected in G1 and G2, with its two worktrees as child rows. SIGNALS_101 shows the proposed interrupted state (section 04). Rows, lamps and bars are Bloom's, unchanged. Version 1 draws G1 with A's three-line cards (S6).

States

Every T3 thread state on a Bloom lamp and bead bar. One new state, three places.

T3 says a chat's state in a coloured word with an icon, and none of them move: working is a dashed ring and a ticking time. Bloom says it by shape first, then motion, then colour: a colourless lamp at the end of the row and the state's colour flowing through a bar of beads. The mapping is close to one to one: two of T3's labels fold into a Bloom state each, and four rows have no T3 label at all.

Bloom's motion stays as built: every loop on the 7 s clock, Reduce motion stills them all. T3's "Input" (Claude asked you a question mid-turn) and "Approval" (a tool waits for your yes) both block the turn, so both are needs-you, the one arm on screen. "Limited" is a turn the usage limit stopped; it reads as failed with the reset time in its words. Bloom's choose on return keeps its lilac for a turn that ended on a question or a plan for you to pick from, which T3 marks only in the chat. Housekeeping is Claude compacting its context, which T3 shows only as a shimmer in the chat. Version 1 draws working and compacting on the background lamp, the band rising through a ring, because both are Claude at work; their bars keep their own colours.

06

Lock

The full lock's four surfaces in version 1: the offer, the sheet, the plate and terminal claude's refusal. Drawn 11 Oct 2026 for the owner's approval.

A lock is a commitment device, not security. It ends at a fixed time and nothing lifts it early. A full lock refuses your sends in every chat, from every device, and refuses new prompts in terminal claude; running turns and agents carry on, and a chat that needs you comes through until you've answered it. A group lock does the same for the groups you tick. Bloom drew v1's lock for the menu bar and tmux (design/bloom, section 07); version 1 has neither, so the surfaces move into the window and into Claude Code's own prompt.

L1The offer

When nothing has needed you for 10 minutes, a note offers a lock in the update note's slot, under the search. Nothing pops: it waits for your next look, which is the moment it's for. Lock opens the sheet set to Full for 2 h, and only the sheet's Lock commits. Later hides the note until something next needs you. It never shows during a lock. A failed chat doesn't count as needing you; an idle one doesn't stop the offer.

L2The lock sheet, ⌘L

⌘L opens the sheet, and so do the offer's Lock and Lock… in a group's menu, which ticks that group. It is the only way in, so the promise is read at the moment it matters. Full covers the window, every chat and terminal claude. Group covers the groups you tick: they start as the groups with a working chat, less the one you're in. For is 30 min, 1 h or 2 h, or a time typed into Until, at most 12 h ahead; the end is fixed the moment you press Lock.

The no-early-unlock sentence is a promise, not a danger, so it is in ink and Lock is the primary. During a lock, ⌘L can only extend it: the presets that would end sooner go, and Lock reads Extend.

L3The lock plate

During a full lock the window is the plate: the logo, the end time, what is still running and one sentence, on white in Field and black in Night. There is no sidebar, panel or composer to get past, and quitting and relaunching lands on it again. The logo is the sidebar's stair drawn in 5 px cells, and a band of shadow crosses it in a 4 × 4 ordered dither, left to right on the 7 s clock, so most of the logo is lit at any moment and the shadow's cells hold at 22 %. Under Reduce motion it holds still and whole. ⌘L on the plate opens the sheet to extend.

L3bBreak-through

When a chat needs you, the plate steps aside for it. The window comes forward without taking focus, so typing in another app can't answer it, and holds only the chats that need you, under a Locked until note in the offer's slot. The chat is open, and you answer in the composer as always. There is no search, panel, settings, fold or usage. Once nothing needs you, the plate comes back. A failure doesn't come through; a question or an approval does.

L4Terminal claude

Terminal claude learns of the lock from a UserPromptSubmit hook in ~/.claude/settings.json, which reads CONTROL's lock file. A refused prompt stays in the box, and Claude Code prints the hook's line in its warning colour. claude -p is refused the same way. CONTROL's own chats pass the hook, because the server already refuses your sends to them and must let agents' messages through. Running turns and permission prompts are untouched: the hook only sees new prompts. Past midnight the time reads 10:00 tomorrow.

Loose ends

What an agent has handed you to do, or is waiting on you to answer, kept with its chat until you've done it. Drawn 12 Oct 2026 for the owner's approval.

Agents end turns with things only you can do: make the forum accounts, send the drafted emails, take a photo of the meter, answer which forums to post on. In a long chat these scroll away. A loose end keeps each one with its chat until you tick it off. Agents file them over CONTROL's MCP; nothing is guessed from a reply. Each chat has its own. The mail icon at the far right of the panel's tab row opens this chat's, and a chat with any open shows the count on its card, so you can see what you owe without opening every chat. Nothing pings: needs-you stays for what is blocking a turn right now.

LE1The popover

The icon opens a popover hung from it, over whatever view the panel shows; it doesn't switch the panel. ⌘E opens and closes it too. Each loose end is one line in the agent's words and a reason under it, with the turn it came from. A click on the turn scrolls the thread to it. An agent can attach one action: a link (Open), a file (Reveal) or a command (Copy). The box ticks it Done; the × on hover dismisses it, for one you won't do. The agent can close one itself when it can see it's done, for instance once you've sent the photos in the chat.

LE2On the cards

Any chat with a loose end open shows the mail glyph and the count on line 2, just before the tokens. A click on the count opens that chat with its popover up. Settled chats keep the mark in the Settled fold. A long chat name gives way to it; on line 3 the state words did, and on line 1 the project name (owner's pick, 12 Oct).

LE3Settling

Every way of settling a chat that still owes you asks first: the menu, ⌘⇧S and a drop on the Settled fold. Settle keeps the loose ends with the chat, and its card keeps the mark in Settled. Esc and Cancel leave it where it was. A chat with nothing open settles as it does today.

LE4The phone

The phone has no panel, so the chat's header carries the mail item beside its list item, with the count as a badge. It opens a sheet with the same list, at half height first, and everything works there: tick, dismiss, Open and Copy. The phone's own reskin is not designed yet (HANDOFF §12); the header items are native (SF Symbols envelope and list.bullet), so only the sheet is CONTROL's own drawing.

08

Local models

Models that run on this Mac, woken when needed and put to sleep when idle, for private data that shouldn't reach Claude. Drawn 12 Oct 2026 for the owner's approval.

A local model is a tool, not a provider: Claude is still the only chat. An agent with private files to process hands them to a local model by path, and only the model's answer comes back, so what the files hold never enters Claude's context. CONTROL starts each model as its own llama-server process, keeps its PID, port and memory on record, and puts it to sleep after 10 idle minutes. A model is asleep (not running), waking (loading, then reading its system prompt once so it's cached) or ready. Before a wake, CONTROL checks free memory and keeps 15 GB back, and anything over 40 GB asks you first. Quitting CONTROL puts every model to sleep, and after a crash CONTROL kills the ones it started, so no model is left eating memory with no one watching it.

M1Awake, in the sidebar

While a model is awake, a note in the update note's form sits under the update note: its lamp, its name and its state, and Sleep. There's one note per awake model, and none while every model is asleep. A click on the name opens Settings › Local models. Sleep ends the process at once. While a model is still waking, Sleep stops the load.

In the thread, a local model is a tool call like any other. The row names the model and what it was given (the count of files, never their contents), and the meta is the time and the answer's length.

M2Settings › Local models

The models you've registered, with what each is doing. An awake row shows its PID, port and resident memory, and when it will sleep. The bar is the Mac's memory: other apps, CONTROL's models, what's free, and the 15 GB kept back, with the swap in use at the end. Selecting a row opens its settings. The command line is filled in from the file when you add it (your serve.sh flags here), and you can edit it. CONTROL owns the host and the port, so the model listens on this Mac only. The system prompt is read once at every wake, so each call after it starts warm. A model sleeps after 10 idle minutes unless you change it. Changes apply at its next wake.

M3Adding a model

Add looks for GGUF files in ~/models and the Hugging Face cache, the format llama-server runs. A vision projector found beside a model is attached to it, not listed on its own. Browse picks any other file. Adding and editing are desktop only, because a model's command runs on the Mac.

M4Waking from a chat

An agent wakes a model when it calls one that's asleep: the call waits through the wake, then runs. Over 40 GB, the wake asks you first, in that chat, the way a permission request does: the chat needs you until you answer, and only your Approve wakes it. If the wake would leave less than 15 GB free, or the Mac is already using swap, CONTROL refuses it, the agent gets the numbers back, and nothing else is touched. CONTROL doesn't put other models to sleep to make room.

M5The phone

The phone shows the same note at the top of the chat list while a model is awake, and Settings › Local models lists every model with Wake and Sleep. The same memory check and the same over-40 GB ask apply there. Adding a model and editing its command are on the Mac only.

Menu bar

The menu-bar icon and its panel, and the ⌘K button in the chat's head. Drawn and approved by the owner 16 Oct 2026.

The desktop app is a window and a menu-bar item in one process, and Quit in the menu bar stops the server and every agent (HANDOFF-v2 §4). The icon is the one ambient signal CONTROL has: by its shape alone it says whether anything needs you, is running, or finished while you were away, and nothing pings. A click opens the panel under it, with what needs you, what's running and what's done, your usage, and Lock, Open, Settings and Quit. The phone and the browser have no menu bar, so this is the desktop app's alone.

MB1The icon

macOS draws a menu-bar icon as a template: one ink, recoloured for a dark or a light bar, so the icon carries no colour and no motion. It is the sidebar logo's stair at 18 pt, and each state changes its shape. The number beside it is the menu bar's own text, the count of chats in the state it shows, so the app needs one image per state, not one per number. When several states hold, the first of these wins: server off, needs you, full lock, failed, done, working, idle. A chat that needs you shows even during a full lock, because it comes through the lock. The icon changes only once a state has held for 2 s, so a turn that ends and starts again doesn't flicker it.

MB2The panel

A click on the icon opens the panel under it; a second click, Esc or a click anywhere else closes it. CONTROL never opens it for you. It is 360 wide and floats like the Loose ends popover. Under the header are the sidebar's notes: the Claude update, and the lock offer once nothing has needed you for 10 minutes. Then three sections of the sidebar's own cards. Needs you holds chats waiting on an answer, including choose on return. Working holds every chat with a turn running. Done holds chats that finished or failed since you last opened them; opening one clears it from here. A click on a card brings the window forward with that chat open. Your usage sits at the foot, as in the sidebar, then Lock, Open, Settings and Quit. Quit is the app's own Quit.

During a full lock the panel holds only chats that need you, which come through the lock as they do in the window, and one line says what's behind it, as the plate does. Lock turns into the end time: nothing in the panel ends a lock. A group lock hides that group's chats, as the window does, and a note in the offer's slot names the group and the time.

MB3Turning it off

Settings › General has a Menu bar icon switch, on by default. Off takes the icon and its panel away; the window and the app menu's Quit stay. The row is in the desktop app only.

MB4The ⌘K button

The palette gets a button in the chat's head, just left of the panel toggles, with their 32 px box, 16 px glyph and hover fill. Its glyph is the ⌘ key, so it doesn't read as the sidebar's search, which finds chats. A click opens the palette, as ⌘K does; its tooltip reads Command palette (⌘K).
