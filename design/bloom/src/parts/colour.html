02

Colour

A neutral night, a cool field, and state colour that flows. Every number computed in OKLCH.

Bloom's palette is three softened primaries, a warm cream and a cool navy. CONTROL sets them on two grounds: Field, the reel's own cool #D6DEEE and the default, and Night, a plain near-black grey whose wells and inputs step down in the same grey. State colour then has two homes: a bead bar on each session's row, its colour flowing through the beads inside one family, and pearls, for identity and for tallies. The lamp at the end of the bar keeps no hue at all, only its shape and its motion. Arm stays the one signal red, and failed leaves the reds for amber-yellow.

C1What the reel measures as

Every colour here was measured from the reel's 60 frames, one every 0.7 s, and converted to OKLCH. Frame 049 at 34.3 s is the cleanest. The reel is an Instagram upload, so the blocky patches in busier frames are video compression: an artefact, not part of the look.

bloom.md §1

Averages, not plateaus

These are k-means centres, so each is an average of what it covers. The crimson plateau is really #DC0042. The true navy floor is #0A1535: #262B5B mixes navy with cobalt. The plateau cream #ECD6C6 sits at the field's own lightness, L 0.890 against 0.899: the field's warm twin.

The tokens keep the k-means values anyway: every contrast and colour-blind proof in this section was computed on them.

C2Rest, then break

The reel doesn't flow evenly. It rests on one family, then breaks into stripes on its way to the next. Rest takes about 40 % of the cycle and the break about 60 %. Cream makes the bright breaks; navy makes the one dark break, where blue turns to red. The hero ramp keeps that rhythm. A state bar keeps only the rest: it never leaves its family, so it has nothing to break into.

Theory 7.6

Rest versus transition

Bloom rests on one hue, with one family holding 55–75 % of the object; in the stripes between rests no family holds more than 31–43 %.

A state bar is a rest that never ends. It blends inside one family, base to light, back to base, down to deep and back, about a third of a tile nearest each step, with no bridge in it, so it flows without a single band. The window around it stays field and ink. When something needs you, every hero ramp gives way and the needs-you bar is the only arm on screen.

C3A softened triad, and one more hue

Bloom's three hues are RGB, softened. On OKLab's colour plane, hue is the angle and chroma the distance from grey at the centre. The base steps sit 127.4°, 136.5° and 96.1° apart: near sRGB's primaries, never on them. The one place they come close to arm is red, so that is where CONTROL draws a moat. Failed gets a hue of its own, amber-yellow, a long way out of it.

Theory 7.1

Softened primaries as a triad

Three hues about 120° apart are as far apart as three colours can be, so the families never compete for one meaning. Bloom's red is turned 24° toward pink, and each base step stops short of the gamut wall, tone-mapped toward cream above.

That headroom is the signal channel. Arm is the one colour at the wall, and past it on a P3 display.

§11.2

A fourth hue, for one job

Theory 7.10

Why OKLCH

Its lightness is close to perceptually even, so a plateau really is one lightness. Its hue holds as chroma changes (HSL's blue drifts purple), so a family keeps its identity from wash to deep. And a blend in OKLab skips sRGB's dark, dull middle: cobalt to crimson meets at L 0.490 in sRGB and 0.541 in OKLab.

C4Two grounds, two ladders

Field keeps the reel's own cool field. Night is new: a plain near-black grey, #2F2E2E, at chroma 0.001, so nothing on it has to fight a blue cast. Chrome steps up from it in the same grey, and everything sunk steps down in it too: inputs and tracks to #242323, the wells, where the terminal, the chat, code and diffs live, to #1B1A1A. Bloom 2's first cut kept v1's navy, #131528, for the wells; against the grey it read as a blue cast, so it went. The one blue-black left, #060714, is shading: shadows and inset gradients, never a fill. The inks climb from cool to cream-warm. Nothing is pure black or white.

Theory 7.2

Cream and navy as value poles, grey in between

Every Bloom hue sits between two poles: cream above at L 0.837, navy below at 0.312. Night now puts a neutral ground between them. Chrome rises from it in grey (raised 1.14, overlay 1.33, seam 1.63) and everything sunk falls in the same grey (sunken 1.16, the well 1.28), in even steps of about 0.04 L. Occlusion, 1.48, is only ever a shadow. Only selection rises in blue, so a selected row still reads as chosen.

Cream is still today's working ink in disguise: the two share OKLab L 0.837 and differ by ΔE 0.026, about one just-noticeable difference. It works as the light bridge in the hero ramp, and in the wipe when a session changes state; a state bar itself never shows it.

Theory 7.4

A cool field pushes; a neutral one doesn't

on #D6DEEEon #2F2E2E

Both squares are the same cream. A surround pushes what it holds toward its own complement: Field's field sits at h 264.4, so everything on it leans toward h 84.4, yellow. Cream reads creamier, warm hues advance and blues recede. It is also why Field's failed bar reads at all: amber sits on the field's complement.

Night's field has no hue to push, C 0.001, so the colours on it read as themselves. That is what you asked of it.

Theory 7.8

The rim, at 0.60 on the grey

Bloom shades a bead's lower edge toward navy. On a dark ground that edge disappears, so a cream rim gives the silhouette back. On #2F2E2E the old 0.45 rim falls to 2.31–2.69:1 against the field.

At 0.60 it reaches 3.44–3.80:1 on the field, 3.01–3.33:1 on raised and 4.41–4.87:1 in the well: the smallest mix that clears 3:1 everywhere. Field keeps 0.30, 3.99–4.69:1. The material itself is Form F3.

C5Families, and the saturation back

Rose, green, blue and lilac are the chrome families: diff washes, TRACE kinds, focus, links. Amber is new and has one job, failed. The state bars take their own, brighter steps of blue, green and lilac, plus amber, and the chart shows how close each comes to sRGB's wall: near it, never at it. Arm alone goes past.

§7 rule 3

Near the wall, never at it

Theory 7.7

Helmholtz–Kohlrausch

Saturated colours look brighter than their luminance. Arm measures L* 57.7 but reads as L** 70.1, about 12 units brighter, which is why it glows without being the lightest thing on screen: cream is L* 80.7, L** 81.4.

So equal OKLab L is not equal brightness, and the chrome families' base steps are re-solved to one L**.

It also means arm's contrast numbers are a floor, not its strength.

§3.1, §11.1

Blue binds at the light end

At L 0.80, sRGB allows only C 0.101 at h 272 (P3 allows 0.110). Night's light steps are lifted to L 0.825–0.835 so accent text keeps the label tier on every Night surface, and up there blue-light holds C 0.082.

C6The ramp

The signature ramp is Bloom's nine stops held on plateaus, with transitions 3 % wide, so each hue shows at its true chroma for most of its length. It moves one full period every 7 s, linear, and its two ends are the same colour, so the join never shows. It lives only in hero places. Next to state, the safe ramp takes over. Bloom 2 gives the sage plateau back some of its chroma.

Theory 7.11

Isoluminant drift, not a flash

That is why the safe ramp can live anywhere near state without ever flashing. D's needs-you blink swung luminance 4.52:1; it retires, and Bloom's needs-you never dims.

§11.5

Where it comes closest to arm

C7Bridges, not blends

Between two families Bloom never blends. It holds each hue, then crosses a separator: cream between hues of similar lightness, navy where the darkest blue wraps round to the darkest red. Each pair of families has exactly one bridge. A state bar never crosses families: it stays inside one, so it blends smoothly, and the bridges are kept for the crossings, the hero ramp and the wipe when a session changes state.

Theory 7.3

Plateau and band

A straight blend between distant hues cuts across the middle of the colour plane, through grey. Coral to sage bottoms out in a dirty salmon inside arm's arc; grass to periwinkle in grey-teal; cobalt to crimson in purple.

Bloom never shows those midpoints. The eye reads a separator as light or shadow, a deliberate event, and a muddy midpoint as dirt.

§4, §11.3

Inside one family, a blend

A bar's tile is one smooth wave: base, up to light, back to base, down to deep, back to base, about a third of it nearest each step. Needs-you has no light step of its own, so it swings between arm and arm-deep, half each. Nothing in a bar is cream, navy or wine, and nothing holds still on a plateau.

So nothing reads as a band. The steepest 2 px step in any row's bar is ΔE 0.0125 in Field and 0.0124 in Night, against 0.236 and 0.210 at Bloom 2's first cut, at its bridges. The light range inside a bar falls from 6.72–10.01:1 to 1.26–2.60:1: the bar is calm, and its tempo carries urgency.

C8State lives in the bar

Bloom 2 splits the job in two. The lamp says how a session behaves, by shape and motion, in ink. The bead bar beside it says which state it is in, by colour: blue working, red needs you, amber failed, green done, lilac choose on return. Quiet states hold still. Every bar flows on the page's clock at its state's tempo, and every bar of one state shares one phase, so rows never chase each other.

§7, replacing theory 7.5's count

Saturation by area, not by count

The reel is 77 / 16 / 6: field, hues, separators. v1 allowed one saturated element per view; Bloom 2 allows saturated colour anywhere it stays small.

  1. Area. Bars are 5 px beads in rows and a 6 px line for the one session in focus; pearls are 20 px or less. Nothing chromatic is a surface, a panel or text.
  2. Ceiling. Every bar, pearl and accent stays at C 0.181 or less and under 96 % of its sRGB ceiling. Arm alone reaches C 0.225.
  3. Tempo. Needs-you alone at 1.75 s.
  4. One continuous bar per view. Lists use bead bars, or three-bead tips.
  5. Pre-emption stays. While a needs-you is lit, hero ramps turn safe; bars keep their colours.

C9Arm stays the only signal red

A saturated Bloom red at mid lightness is, by construction, close to arm. Unmitigated, Bloom's crimson is ΔE 0.117 from it, red base 0.101 and coral 0.110: 5–6 just-noticeable differences, distinct side by side but confusable at a glance. v1 then made failed a second red. Bloom 2 moves failed out of the reds altogether, to amber-yellow, and keeps the six layers that hold every other red away.

§5.2, revised

Six layers

    C10Diffs, the Bloom pair

    H13 delta B10. H13 puts removed lines on arm's dim wash and added lines on a brighter grey, never green. Bloom sinks removed lines into a rose wash below the well and raises added lines on a sage wash above it, with the marks in each family's colour. Diffs always live in the well: in Night that is #1B1A1A, a step below the grey field, never the field itself. Below, the same T14 write both ways.

    Window W3

    Line numbers stay in the gutter

    In Field, caption ink on the rose wash measures 4.38:1, under its tier. Line numbers therefore live in the sunken gutter, never on the wash. The full matrix is C11.

    C11Contrast

    Every text token against every surface it can land on, set in its own ink on its own surface, so the matrix is also the specimen. Tiers: body 7:1 and |Lc| 75; label 4.5:1 and 60; caption 4.5:1 and 45; emphasis, 13 px and weight 600 or more, 4.5:1 and 45; disabled is exempt. Night's lighter ground cost contrast, so its inks were lifted and every tier re-proved on six surfaces, the well included.

    Theory 7.9

    Light on dark isn't symmetric

    WCAG's ratio overrates light text on dark. Night's ink-4 is 6.12:1 by WCAG but only Lc −53.9 by APCA, so Night's ladder is tuned to APCA and passes WCAG on the way. The grey ground costs Night some headroom: ink-1 is 14.79:1 in the well and 11.53:1 on the field.

    Field pays for its colour. Navy on #D6DEEE is 9.84:1 and Lc 79.9, against 13.30:1 and Lc 99.5 on white; even black would only reach Lc 86.4. So Field's body text is ink-1, the navy itself, with no lighter body grey, and its secondary text stops at ink-2.

    C12Colour-blind check

    Lamps carry no hue now, so no colour-vision deficiency can touch them: ten shapes and ten motions carry state for everyone, and the state word says it outright. The bar adds colour on top. Below, each coloured bar under the three dichromat simulations (Machado 2009, full severity), flowing in its simulated colours.

    §11.4

    State never depends on the bar

    Background shares working's bar and differs by its lamp, a ring; outside by its open beads; idle and gone by having no flow. What survives every simulation is lightness, and the bases are spread from needs-you, the darkest, to failed, the lightest.

    C13Terminal palette

    ANSI's sixteen slots, in the well where the terminal lives. Normals sit at the label plateau and brights a step further from the ground. Green slots carry green, a Bloom delta, and slot 9 is a light clay rather than arm, so a CLI's bright red never wears needs-you.

    §5.8

    Slot 9 leaves arm

    Slot 9 sits ΔE 0.199 from arm in Night and 0.164 in Field. Every Night slot reaches 6.45:1 or better in the well except slot 0; every Field slot reaches 4.47:1 or better.
