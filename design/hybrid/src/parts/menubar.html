Menu bar

The menu-bar icon and its panel, and the ⌘K button in the chat's head. Drawn and approved by the owner 16 Oct 2026.

The desktop app is a window and a menu-bar item in one process, and Quit in the menu bar stops the server and every agent (HANDOFF-v2 §4). The icon is the one ambient signal CONTROL has: by its shape alone it says whether anything needs you, is running, or finished while you were away, and nothing pings. A click opens the panel under it, with what needs you, what's running and what's done, your usage, and Lock, Open, Settings and Quit. The phone and the browser have no menu bar, so this is the desktop app's alone.

MB1The icon

macOS draws a menu-bar icon as a template: one ink, recoloured for a dark or a light bar, so the icon carries no colour and no motion. It is the sidebar logo's stair at 18 pt, and each state changes its shape. The number beside it is the menu bar's own text, the count of chats in the state it shows, so the app needs one image per state, not one per number. When several states hold, the first of these wins: server off, needs you, full lock, failed, done, working, idle. A chat that needs you shows even during a full lock, because it comes through the lock. The icon changes only once a state has held for 2 s, so a turn that ends and starts again doesn't flicker it.

MB2The panel

A click on the icon opens the panel under it; a second click, Esc or a click anywhere else closes it. CONTROL never opens it for you. It is 360 wide and floats like the Loose ends popover. Under the header are the sidebar's notes: the Claude update, and the lock offer once nothing has needed you for 10 minutes. Then three sections of the sidebar's own cards. Needs you holds chats waiting on an answer, including choose on return. Working holds every chat with a turn running. Done holds chats that finished or failed since you last opened them; opening one clears it from here. A click on a card brings the window forward with that chat open. Your usage sits at the foot, as in the sidebar, then Lock, Open, Settings and Quit. Quit is the app's own Quit.

During a full lock the panel holds only chats that need you, which come through the lock as they do in the window, and one line says what's behind it, as the plate does. Lock turns into the end time: nothing in the panel ends a lock. A group lock hides that group's chats, as the window does, and a note in the offer's slot names the group and the time.

MB3Turning it off

Settings › General has a Menu bar icon switch, on by default. Off takes the icon and its panel away; the window and the app menu's Quit stay. The row is in the desktop app only.

MB4The ⌘K button

The palette gets a button in the chat's head, just left of the panel toggles, with their 32 px box, 16 px glyph and hover fill. Its glyph is the ⌘ key, so it doesn't read as the sidebar's search, which finds chats. A click opens the palette, as ⌘K does; its tooltip reads Command palette (⌘K).
